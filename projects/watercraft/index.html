Marine Engineering

High-Speed Watercraft

Concept

Exploring the design of a compact, high-speed watercraft using advanced propulsion concepts, water-jet technology, lightweight structures, and innovative hull design.

The concept

This project studies a compact, high-speed watercraft: a small hull that can move efficiently, stay controllable, and stay light. It is in the concept stage. No full-size hull has been built yet.

The work so far is design thinking: hull shape, water-jet intake placement, structural layout, and how a small craft should behave at speed without becoming unstable.

Hull performance

A high-speed hull has to manage two jobs at once: generate lift as speed rises, and keep enough grip in the water to turn. A sharp entry reduces slamming. A planing surface further aft lets the craft rise and shed drag.

The compact size makes those choices stricter. There is less length to spread waves across, so the hull must be efficient in a short envelope.

Propulsion direction

Water-jet propulsion is the current study path. A jet keeps rotating hardware inside the hull, which is safer in shallow water and easier to package in a small body. The trade-off is intake design: the duct has to stay wetted, avoid ingesting air, and not add too much weight.

  • Compact hull with a planing aft section
  • Water-jet intake and nozzle layout
  • Lightweight structure and component mounting
  • Control surfaces and directional stability

Next work

The next useful step is a small-scale hull model for tank or pool observation: how it sits, how it rises, and whether the intake stays submerged. That test would inform a later prototype, the same way the drone frame came before motors.
