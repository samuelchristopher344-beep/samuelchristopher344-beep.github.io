Propulsion

Hybrid Propulsion Systems

Research

Studying combinations of mechanical, electric, and alternative propulsion technologies for future transportation systems.

Why hybrid

Most machines I care about — drones, watercraft, future vehicles — live between two constraints: energy density and control. Electric drive is clean and precise. Mechanical systems can be rugged and simple. Hybrid thinking asks when those should work together instead of competing.

This is a research project. I am mapping combinations, not claiming a finished engine.

Study areas

  • Electric motors for precise thrust and easy electronic control
  • Mechanical reduction and torque paths for compact packages
  • Ducted fans, water-jets, and propeller layouts
  • Battery placement and thermal limits on small prototypes

Connection to current builds

The drone will be a first electric flight system: motors, ESCs, battery, and a controller. That build is the practical classroom for propulsion. The watercraft concept asks a different question — how to push water efficiently in a small hull.

Comparing those two problems is the point of this page. Same energy, different medium, different mechanical interface.

Open questions

How small can a hybrid package get before losses eat the advantage? When is a simple electric propeller the honest answer? Those questions will be answered by tests, not by a render.
